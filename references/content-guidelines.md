# Content guidelines

Contents: Section order · Writing bullets · Summary · Tailoring to a posting · Proofreading checklist · Technical profiles

## Section order

Default: Summary → Skills → Experience → Education → Certifications → Projects → Languages.

Adjust to the person:
- **Early career / career changers**: Education and Projects move above Experience.
- **Experienced (5+ years)**: Experience first after the summary; Education short.
- **Certification-heavy fields** (security, cloud, IT): put Certifications right after Skills if they are central to the target role.
- A skills block near the top helps keyword matching, but keep it honest and grouped, not a 40-item dump.

## Writing bullets

Aim for: **action verb + what you did + how/with what + result**.

- Weak: "Responsible for maintaining camera systems."
- Better: "Maintained 400+ IP cameras across 12 sites, cutting downtime 30% through scheduled firmware and health checks."

Rules of thumb:
- 3–6 bullets per recent role, fewer for older ones. Lead with the strongest.
- One line is ideal, two lines max.
- Quantify where the user can supply real numbers (scale, speed, savings, uptime, team size). If they can't, describe scope instead (systems, sites, users). Never invent numbers; ask.
- Past tense for past roles, present tense for the current role. Keep it consistent within a role.
- No first-person pronouns, no "responsible for", no "helped with" without saying what the help was.
- Don't repeat the same opening verb in a role.

## Summary

Two to four lines: who you are professionally, years/domain, 2–3 strongest differentiators, and the direction you're aiming (matching the target role). No adjectives like "passionate" or "results-driven" that anyone could write. Student or career-changer versions lead with training and relevant projects.

## Tailoring to a posting

1. **Extract** from the posting: job title, must-have skills/tools, nice-to-haves, repeated phrases, and domain keywords.
2. **Match honestly**: map each requirement to real evidence in the CV. Use the posting's wording when it genuinely describes what the person did (e.g. "access control" vs "door entry systems").
3. **Adjust**:
   - Rewrite the summary toward the target role.
   - Reorder skills and bullets so the most relevant come first; trim what's irrelevant.
   - Update the headline `title` to match the role.
   - Do not add skills or experience that aren't real.
4. **Report gaps**: list requirements with no evidence in the CV, and suggest the user confirm whether they have that experience (they may simply not have mentioned it) before you add anything.
5. Save as a separate file; keep the master CV intact.

## Proofreading checklist

Fix silently, and summarise the notable changes afterwards:
- Spelling and grammar; one English variant throughout (ask or follow the user's existing choice).
- Tense consistency and parallel structure across bullets.
- Date format consistency (`Mar 2022 – Present`), and no unexplained gaps or overlaps (flag these, don't fix them).
- Consistent capitalisation of tools and products (GitHub, PostgreSQL, Wi-Fi).
- Remove filler ("various", "etc.", "responsible for"), clichés, and duplicated points.
- Check contact details are plausible and links are formatted consistently.

Do not change facts while polishing. If a sentence is ambiguous, ask what was meant instead of guessing.

## Technical profiles (IT, security, engineering)

- Group skills by category (e.g. Languages, Tools, Platforms, Standards) rather than one long list.
- Certifications: name, issuer, year; mark in-progress ones as "in progress (expected 2026)".
- Projects/labs: one line on purpose, one on tools and outcome. Keep them to work the user is free to disclose.
- Mind confidentiality: don't name clients, internal hostnames, or sensitive findings from engagements. Describe scope generically ("internal network assessment for a financial-sector client").
- Cross-domain experience (e.g. physical security + cybersecurity) is a strength; connect the two explicitly in the summary rather than leaving the reader to infer it.
